# Clinical review queue

Everything in `clinical/` that an engineer authored and a clinician has not yet
confirmed. This is the agenda for the AIIA mentor session — a list of questions
about clinical content, not a list of known defects.

Two things worth stating before the session:

- **Nothing here is a diagnostic rule.** This system records what a patient
  reports and what their documents say. It never states a diagnosis, never gives
  advice, and never decides anything about a patient's care. The interaction
  table below flags a pair for a physician to look at; it does not recommend
  stopping anything.
- **Every item can be changed by editing `clinical/**` alone.** None of it needs
  a code change. That is the point of keeping the content out of Python, and it
  means a Vaidya can review and correct these in a pull request.

Compiled by hand on 2026-09-05, extended the same day with the patient app's
translations (items 1 and 2), and again on 2026-09-06 with the dashboard's Hindi
strings (item 3a). The previous version was generated by
`scripts/review_queue.py`, which read the pathway and red-flag YAML that now
lives in `backend/stale/` — the interview runs on the Jetson and this repository
no longer holds those files.

## 1. App UI translations — 65 strings × 8 non-English languages

`app/lib/l10n/app_*.arb`

**Engineering drafts.** Written by an engineer, not by native speakers, and not
by clinicians. Every ARB carries every key and the build fails otherwise
(`app/test/l10n_completeness_test.dart`), so what is missing is not coverage —
it is correctness.

These are UI chrome, not clinical prompts: the questions themselves come from the
bundle and exist only in English and Hindi (see item 2). But four of these
strings have clinical meaning defined by the record contract, and a wrong
translation there produces a **wrong clinical status**, not an awkward phrase:

- [ ] **`answerDontKnow`** — records `unresolved`. Must read as "I cannot say",
      and must **never** read as "no". A patient who reads it as a refusal, or as
      a negative answer, produces a record stating something they did not say.
- [ ] **`answerSkip`** — records `not_asked`. Must be visibly different from
      `answerDontKnow` in every script; they are different medico-legal
      positions and a patient who cannot tell them apart will pick the wrong one.
- [ ] **`consentNotTranslated`** — shown when the consent notice has no text in
      the language the patient chose, which today is every language but English
      and Hindi. It is the one string whose *job* is to be read by somebody the
      app has just refused to serve, so it names the two available languages in
      their own scripts (`English`, `हिन्दी`) rather than only describing them.
      Check that the sentence around those two words reads as an apology and an
      instruction, not as an error.
- [ ] **`skipForNow`** — leaves the optional ABHA screen. It must **not** read
      like `answerSkip`, which skips a clinical question and records
      `not_asked`; these are unrelated actions that happen to share an English
      word, and a language that renders them identically invites a patient to
      believe declining ABHA declines a question.
- [ ] **`optYes` / `optNo`** — the only two strings in the app that become a
      boolean in the record.

Then the urgent-care screen (`urgentTitle`, `urgentBody`, `urgentAction`), which
is the highest-stakes text in the app:

- [ ] Does it read as an instruction to seek care **now**, in each language, and
      not as general advice a patient might defer?
- [ ] Does it name no condition and suggest no diagnosis? Automated checks cover
      Latin-script disease names only; a native speaker has to confirm the
      indigenous-script text says nothing diagnostic.
- [ ] **The emergency number.** `108` is the default and is not universal across
      Indian states. Confirm it per deployment; it is a per-hospital field, not a
      constant.

Languages: Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, Punjabi.

## 2. Clinical question prompts — English and Hindi only

`clinical/questions/**`

The bundle advertises `en` and `hi` because those are the languages the
questions are authored in, and the backend refuses to advertise a language it
has no prompts for. Where a patient chooses one of the other seven, the app
records `not_asked` rather than falling back to English.

**Do not close this gap by machine-translating the prompts.** A question that
shifts meaning between languages silently changes what the record means, and no
test can catch it. Each language needs a clinician who speaks it.

- [ ] Decide which languages to author next, and who authors them.
- [ ] Until then, confirm that recording `not_asked` for a patient who chose an
      unsupported language is the behaviour the hospital wants — the alternative
      is refusing those languages at the picker, which is more honest but offers
      the patient nothing.

## 3. The Hindi report wording — 36 strings, 14 sections

`clinical/report_templates/hi/report.yaml`

**The highest-value item on this list.** The Hindi file is written as Hindi
rather than translated from the English at runtime, and the two are allowed to
differ where clinical Hindi differs from clinical English. It was written by an
engineer working from reference material, which is not the same as written by a
clinician who speaks to patients in Hindi every day.

Read as a whole, out loud, by somebody who does. The specific questions:

- [ ] Does `not_established` read as *"यह जानकारी प्राप्त नहीं हुई"* — the
      information was not obtained — and never as a negative? A line that reads
      as "the patient does not have this" where the truth is "nobody asked" is
      the single worst failure this document can have.
- [ ] Are `unresolved`, `not_asked`, `not_applicable` and `refused` four
      visibly different statements in Hindi? They are four different
      medico-legal positions and the English file keeps them apart; the Hindi
      must too.
- [ ] Is the header disclaimer — that this is patient-reported, unverified, and
      contains no diagnosis — phrased the way a physician would expect to see it?
- [ ] Are the section headings the words used in an AYUSH OPD, or the words a
      dictionary gives?

The English file (`en/report.yaml`, same 36 strings) has had eyes on it and is
the reference for meaning, not for phrasing.

## 3a. The dashboard's Hindi interface strings — 170 strings

`dashboard/src/i18n/strings.ts`

**Engineering drafts**, like items 1 and 2. Written by an engineer, not by a
native speaker and not by a clinician. The file is typed so a missing string is a
compile error, and a test asserts no English string is sitting in the Hindi
catalogue — so what is missing is not coverage, it is correctness.

These are the doctor's chrome, not the record: a transcript, a report line, a
section title, a conflict statement and a red-flag label all arrive from the
backend in the interview's language and are never translated here (decision 64).
But several of these strings name a clinical act, and a wrong word changes what a
control means:

- [ ] **`verify.reject` and `verify.rejectWarning`** — "अस्वीकारें" records that
      a fact was **never established**, not that the answer is no. The warning
      shown before confirming has to make that unmistakable in Hindi, because a
      physician who reads it as "mark this false" will use it to record a
      negative finding, which is the one thing the record model exists to
      prevent.
- [ ] **`verify.accept` vs `verify.amend`** — confirming what is written versus
      typing something different. Both write a physician's name to the audit log
      and only one of them counts towards the correction rate.
- [ ] **`state.red_flag_pending`** ("तत्काल समीक्षा मानदंड") and
      **`alerts.title`** — must read as *a criterion was met and a human should
      look now*, never as a finding about the patient.
- [ ] **`alerts.acknowledge` vs `alerts.escalate`** — acknowledging records that
      you looked; escalating is something a clinician does in person and this
      only records that they did. If the two read as near-synonyms in Hindi, the
      §1 rule 2 separation is defeated in the UI even though it holds in the API.
- [ ] **`report.draft`** — "मसौदा रिपोर्ट — चिकित्सक सत्यापन आवश्यक". The one
      sentence on the screen that says this document is not yet a clinical
      record.
- [ ] **`report.unresolved`** ("अनिर्णीत") — must not read as "pending" in the
      administrative sense, or as "negative".

Not on this list, deliberately: the fact-state labels in
`dashboard/src/report/factState.ts` ("not established", "reconstructed from
unclear input"). They are part of the document's meaning rather than its
furniture and are currently English in both locales; translating them is a
clinical-wording decision and belongs with item 3.

## 4. The drug–drug interaction table — 20 rules

`clinical/interactions/seed.yaml`

Every row carries a source and a row without one does not load. The sources are
the WHO Model Formulary 2008 monographs and the CDSCO *National Formulary of
India* 6th ed. What has **not** been reviewed is the *selection*: whether these
twenty are the right twenty for an Indian AYUSH OPD population, and whether any
severity is pitched wrongly.

**Scope, deliberately:** drug–drug only, among medicines the patient is already
taking. **There is no herb–drug prediction and none should be added without
evidence** — this is exactly where a plausible false positive would have a
physician stop a patient's Ayurvedic medicine on the authority of a kiosk.

## 8. The consent notice — 1 version, 4 purposes

`clinical/consent/consent_v1.yaml`

`clinical_source` records it as *"drafted against DPDP Act 2023 notice
requirements; pending institutional legal review"*.

- [ ] **Institutional legal review**, not clinical review. The DPDP Act requires
      that we can produce, for any patient, exactly what they agreed to, in
      which language, when, and who granted it — which is why the text is
      versioned content rather than a string in a template.
- [ ] Is the Hindi notice text right? Same concern as item 1, with legal weight.
- [ ] `raw_audio_retention` is a separately granted purpose and is off unless
      the patient grants it. Confirm that separation is what the institution
      wants.
- [ ] **`applies_to: [kiosk]` on `raw_audio_retention`.** The patient app has no
      microphone, so `GET /content/consent?source=app` omits that purpose
      entirely. The reasoning is that asking a patient to consent to the
      retention of audio the app cannot capture would file an artefact
      describing something that never happened — for every app intake. Confirm
      that omitting it is preferable to showing it as permanently refused; both
      are defensible and the choice is the institution's.
- [ ] **The notice exists in English and Hindi only.** A patient who chose one
      of the other seven UI languages is stopped at the consent screen rather
      than shown the notice in a language they did not choose (see item 1,
      `consentNotTranslated`). That is the safe failure, but it means seven of
      the nine languages cannot complete an intake in the app until this notice
      is translated. It is the single highest-value translation on this page.

## 8a. Carrying a confirmed fact forward has no place in the contract

`app/lib/submit/record.dart`, decision 54

Screen 5 asks a returning patient whether the hospital's record is still
correct. A Yes means the interview does not ask that question again, and the
field is submitted `answered` with `source_turn: null` — which says "nothing in
this interview asked it" but does not say where it came from.

- [x] **Is `source_turn: null` enough provenance for a physician?** *Answered:
      no.* Schema **0.2** adds `carried_forward` to a field entry —
      `from_intake_id`, `originally_recorded`, and a three-valued
      `confirmed_today` where `null` means the question was not put today. The
      dashboard renders it as "from a previous visit", with the date and whether
      the patient confirmed it, and the evidence panel links to that visit.
      0.1 keeps working. The clinical question that remains is below.
- [ ] **Is "confirmed today" the right thing to record, or is "re-asked today"?**
      They differ when a patient contradicts a carried fact rather than
      confirming it. The contract can express that (`confirmed_today: false`)
      and the app does not currently produce it, because screen 5 offers "still
      correct?" rather than re-asking the question. Which of the two a physician
      wants is a clinical call.
- [ ] Confirm that "still correct?" per item is the right granularity. A patient
      with eleven carried facts sees eleven rows before their first question.

## 8b. The Ayurveda current-state subset

`clinical/questions/ayurveda/ayurveda_module.yaml`, `current_state: true`

2/3 §5 screen 8 asks for the full module on a first visit and a current-state
subset on a return, naming "digestion, appetite, sleep, bowel habit". That has
been mapped to three of the nine fields — `agni_appetite` (digestion and
appetite are one question here), `nidra_sleep`, `koshtha_bowel`.

- [ ] **Is that the right three?** `mutra_urine` and `sweda_perspiration` are
      also current-state observations rather than constitutional ones, and a
      Vaidya may well want them at every visit. The mapping was made from the
      brief's wording, not from clinical judgement.
- [ ] **Is a fortnight the right assumption?** The subset applies to any patient
      this hospital has seen before, with no interval test. A patient returning
      after two years arguably deserves the full module again.

## 9. The document blur threshold

`app/lib/documents/prepare.dart`

Not a clinical judgement, but it has a clinical consequence and nobody has
calibrated it. The variance-of-Laplacian threshold is set at 800, separating
synthetic fixtures that score ~11000 sharp and ~250 blurred. Real prescriptions
on cream paper under OPD fluorescent light will not score like that.

- [ ] Tune against a corpus of real photographs. Erring low is deliberate —
      refusing a usable photograph sends a patient to re-photograph a document
      for no reason, and they will not do it twice.

## Not on this list

The pathways, the red-flag rules and the coverage model. They were reviewed
items in the previous build and they are no longer this service's concern: the
interview, question selection and red-flag evaluation run on the Jetson at the
kiosk. This backend receives already-fired red flags as events and persists
them; it does not evaluate rules. See `backend/stale/README.md`.
